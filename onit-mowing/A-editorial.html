North, west and central Auckland

Care

Weekly mowing

The lawn, cut on a standing day. Catcher on, edges run, clippings off the paths. Same crew, same height, so the grass does not bounce between long and scalped.

Hedges and trim

Sides and tops kept to the line you already have. We do not invent a new shape. We hold the one that belongs to the house, then blow the cuttings off the beds.

Edges and berms

The cut is only half the job. We run the line at the path, the drive, and the berm, so the lawn reads as a plane instead of a fray.

Garden care

A tidy of the beds, a bag of mulch, a few plants in the gap that has been bothering you. Small work, done on the same visit as the mow when it fits the round.

Property rounds

For managers who need a street of sections held to one standard. We quote the round, keep a day, and send a note if something on site needs a second look.

The quiet week

If you are away, the lawn still gets done. If it rains, we shift the day, not the height. The point of a weekly is that you stop thinking about it.

Note 01

The lawn is a surface.

It should read as one plane from the house to the fence. Height even, clippings caught, no ridge where the mower turned. That is the first thing we look at when we walk on.

Note 02

The line is the hedge.

A hedge that has been let go takes a season to bring back. A hedge held every few weeks stays a wall. We cut to the existing face, not to a fashion.

Note 03

The week is the contract.

Fortnightly looks cheaper until January. A weekly round in Auckland, through growth and drought, is how a section stays the way you wanted it in spring.

Work

Recent passes. Lawns, beds, and the sort of planting that sits quietly against a fence. Auckland light, ordinary sections, finished for the week.

The round

Auckland grows in weeks, not seasons.

Spring on the North Shore is not a mood. It is a lawn that puts on two centimetres between Thursdays. A fortnightly that looked tidy in August is a hay paddock by Labour Weekend. We quote the weekly because that is the interval that holds a section in this climate.

West Auckland clay, central shade, coastal wind — the cut height stays the same. What changes is whether we catch, whether we edge the berm, and whether the hedge is on this visit or the next. You should not have to remember which.

Who it is for

Homes that want the week back. Managers who want one standard.

Most of the round is ordinary houses: Grey Lynn, Mount Eden, Ponsonby, Westmere, Takapuna, Titirangi. Gate on the side, dog in the back, bins out on the wrong morning. We work around that.

The rest is property managers who need a street of rentals to look like one job. We keep a day, we send a note if a tenant’s hedge is out, and we do not renegotiate the round every October.
